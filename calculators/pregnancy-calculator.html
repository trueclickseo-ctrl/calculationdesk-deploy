Introduction

Pregnancy Calculator – Track Gestational Age & Milestones

Preparing for a new arrival is an extraordinary journey. From the moment you receive a positive test, tracking your pregnancy week-by-week helps you understand how your baby is developing and what changes to expect.

This calculator inputs the first day of your Last Menstrual Period (LMP) and generates your complete gestational timeline:

  1. Gestational Age: Progress displayed in weeks and days.
  2. Estimated Due Date (EDD): Calculated at 40 weeks (280 days).
  3. Estimated Conception Date: Estimated at LMP + 14 days.
  4. Trimester Stage: First, Second, or Third Trimester.
  5. Baby Size Analogy: Educational comparison of baby growth to familiar fruits and vegetables.
Warm Medical Disclaimer: This pregnancy tracker provides educational estimations based on standard 40-week gestational rules. It is not a substitute for professional medical care, prenatal clinical exams, or diagnostic ultrasounds. Always consult your obstetrician, midwife, or doctor for personalized healthcare.

How Pregnancy Weeks Are Counted

In clinical obstetrics, pregnancy is dated from the first day of your Last Menstrual Period (LMP) rather than the date of conception:

  • Weeks 1 and 2: Your body is preparing for ovulation. You are not technically pregnant yet, but these two weeks are included in the standard 40-week count.
  • Week 2 to 3: Conception typically occurs around day 14 of a standard 28-day cycle when a sperm fertilizes the released egg.
  • Weeks 3 to 4: Implantation occurs in the uterine lining.

Trimester Progress Breakdown

  • First Trimester (Weeks 1 to 12): Implantation, rapid cell division, organ formation, and first heartbeat.
  • Second Trimester (Weeks 13 to 26): Fetal growth, early movements ("quickening"), and hearing development.
  • Third Trimester (Weeks 27 to 40): Lung maturation, rapid weight gain, brain activity, and birth preparation.

Why Ultrasound Dates May Differ

If your doctor performs a first-trimester dating ultrasound, the scan date may differ slightly from your LMP calculation:

  • LMP calculations assume a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14.
  • If you ovulated earlier or later than day 14, an ultrasound measuring the baby's crown-rump length (CRL) provides a more precise clinical due date.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Q1: Why does pregnancy age start 2 weeks before conception occurs?
  • A1: Because the exact day of ovulation and fertilization is difficult to pinpoint in natural conception, obstetricians worldwide use the first day of your Last Menstrual Period (LMP) as a reliable standardized starting marker.
  • Q2: What is the difference between an LMP due date and an ultrasound due date?
  • A2: An LMP calculator assumes a standard 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. A first-trimester dating ultrasound measures the embryo's crown-rump length (CRL) directly, which can adjust the due date if your cycle was shorter or longer than average.
  • Q3: How are pregnancy trimesters divided?
  • A3: First Trimester: Weeks 1 through 12. Second Trimester: Weeks 13 through 26. Third Trimester: Weeks 27 through 40.
