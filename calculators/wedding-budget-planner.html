Introduction

Wedding Budget Planner

This planner takes a single number — your total wedding budget — and splits it across six commonly used categories: venue and catering, photography and video, attire, entertainment, flowers and decor, and a contingency reserve for the costs that aren't obvious upfront.

These percentages reflect commonly cited industry averages, not a rule you need to follow — every category in the calculator is adjustable, so if photography matters more to you than flowers, you can shift the split accordingly and the dollar amounts update to match.

Why the contingency line matters

Wedding budgets tend to run into costs that don't show up in an initial vendor quote — service charges, delivery fees, alterations, permits, and vendor tips all add up. Holding back 10-15% of the total as a dedicated contingency reserve, rather than allocating the full budget across the other five categories, gives you room to absorb those without going over budget or cutting something you actually wanted.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q:What is the single most expensive wedding category?

Venue rental and catering together usually account for 40-50% of the total budget, and guest count is the single biggest driver of that cost — cutting the guest list is typically the most effective lever for reducing overall spend.

Q:Why is a contingency/miscellaneous budget necessary?

Wedding planning tends to surface costs that aren't obvious in the initial vendor quotes — service charges, local permit fees, vendor gratuities, attire alterations, taxes, and postage. Setting aside 10-15% upfront means these don't blow the rest of the budget when they show up.

Q:How should we allocate a tight wedding budget?

Identify your top two or three genuine priorities — often a specific venue, photographer, or food quality — and fund those closer to full price first, then trim the categories that matter less to you (favors, elaborate stationery, and similar extras are common places to cut).

Q:Is tipping wedding vendors customary?

Yes, for many vendor roles — hair and makeup, bartenders, delivery and setup crews, and drivers commonly expect a tip unless gratuity is already built into the contract. Check each vendor's contract, since practices vary.
