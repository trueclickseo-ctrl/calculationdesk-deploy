Introduction

Target Heart Rate Calculator – Training Zones & Karvonen Guide

Whether training for a 10K, building cardiovascular endurance, optimizing workouts for fat burn, or pacing recovery runs, exercising in the right heart rate zone helps get the maximum benefit from cardio sessions without overtraining.

Target heart rate is the ideal range of heartbeats per minute (bpm) at which the heart and lungs are conditioned effectively. This calculator computes Maximum Heart Rate (MHR), Heart Rate Reserve (HRR), and 5 training intensity zones using two approaches: the simple Standard Method and the more personalized Karvonen Method.

Exercise safety disclaimer: Heart rate formulas provide general population estimates. Individual maximum heart rate can vary by roughly 10 to 15 bpm from the formula's prediction. Anyone experiencing chest pain, severe shortness of breath, or dizziness, or who takes heart medication such as beta-blockers, should stop exercising immediately and consult a physician.

Standard Method vs. Karvonen Method

The Standard Method relies solely on age to estimate Maximum Heart Rate (MHR), using the classic Åstrand formula:

MHR = 220 - Age qquad Target Heart Rate = MHR × Intensity Percentage

For a 30-year-old (MHR = 190 bpm), Zone 3 (70% to 80%) equals a lower bound of 190 × 0.70 = 133 bpm and an upper bound of 190 × 0.80 = 152 bpm.

The Karvonen Method accounts for resting heart rate (RHR) — the beats per minute the heart pumps when completely relaxed — establishing a Heart Rate Reserve (HRR):

HRR = MHR - RHR qquad Target Heart Rate = RHR + (HRR × Intensity Percentage)

The Standard method assumes everyone of the same age shares the same fitness baseline. The Karvonen method recognizes that a fit runner with an RHR of 50 bpm has a larger usable heart rate reserve (140 bpm) than an inactive individual of the same age with an RHR of 80 bpm (110 bpm reserve) — shifting the zones higher for fitter individuals to reflect their true physiological conditioning.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Karvonen Method, Age 30, RHR 60 bpm

MHR = 220 - 30 = 190 bpm. HRR = 190 - 60 = 130 bpm.

Zone 3 Aerobic (70%–80%): lower bound = 60 + (130 × 0.70) = 60 + 91 = 151 bpm; upper bound = 60 + (130 × 0.80) = 60 + 104 = 164 bpm.

This gives a Zone 3 Aerobic range of 151 to 164 bpm — noticeably higher than the Standard method's 133–152 bpm for the same age, since this person's below-average resting heart rate expands their reserve.

Example 2: Standard Method, Age 45

MHR = 220 - 45 = 175 bpm.

Zone 2 Fat Burn (60%–70%): lower bound = 175 × 0.60 = 105 bpm; upper bound = 175 × 0.70 = 122.5 to 122 bpm.

Zone 4 Anaerobic (80%–90%): lower bound = 175 × 0.80 = 140 bpm; upper bound = 175 × 0.90 = 157.5 to 158 bpm.

Practical Workout Guidance

Wearing a chest strap or optical smartwatch monitor during runs or workouts helps maintain a target bpm in real time. A common training principle is to spend roughly 80% of weekly cardio volume in Zone 2 to build aerobic base without burning out, reserving Zone 4 and Zone 5 for short interval sessions once or twice a week.

When to Seek Medical Guidance

Consult a doctor or exercise physiologist rather than relying solely on a calculator when taking medications that affect heart rate (such as beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, or asthma inhalers), when returning to exercise after a cardiac event, surgery, or prolonged illness, or when experiencing irregular heart rhythms (palpitations or flutters) during physical activity.

What This Calculator Does Not Include

Real-world exclusions: Both formulas are population averages based on age (and resting heart rate, for Karvonen). They do not account for individual genetics, medication effects, altitude, heat, caffeine, or measurement error in resting heart rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the Karvonen method preferred by fitness trainers?

The Karvonen formula incorporates resting heart rate (RHR), which reflects personal cardiorespiratory fitness level. A fitter person with a lower resting heart rate has a larger Heart Rate Reserve, shifting training boundaries to match their actual aerobic capacity.

What is the "Fat Burn" zone and does it burn more total fat?

Zone 2 (60% to 70% intensity) relies primarily on fat oxidation for fuel. While fat provides a higher percentage of calories burned in Zone 2, higher-intensity workouts in Zone 3 and Zone 4 burn more total calories per minute.

Can medications affect target heart rate zones?

Yes. Medications such as beta-blockers, blood pressure drugs, and thyroid treatments can significantly suppress or elevate heart rate. Anyone taking cardiac medication should consult their doctor for personalized heart rate guidelines rather than relying on the standard formulas.

Why do the Standard and Karvonen methods give different heart rates for the same person?

The Standard method calculates zones purely as a percentage of Maximum Heart Rate, ignoring fitness level. The Karvonen method starts from resting heart rate and scales only the "reserve" capacity above it, which shifts every zone boundary upward for someone with a lower, fitter resting heart rate.
