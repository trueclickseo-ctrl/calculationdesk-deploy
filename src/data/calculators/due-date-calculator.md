# Pregnancy Due Date Calculator – LMP, Conception & IVF Guide

Finding out you are pregnant is an exciting milestone. One of the first questions expecting parents ask is: **"When is the baby due?"**

An **Estimated Due Date (EDD)** marks the estimated time when a full-term pregnancy reaches **40 weeks (280 days)**. 

This calculator supports **3 clinical calculation modes**:
1. **Last Menstrual Period (LMP) Mode**: Uses Naegele's Rule adjusted for your average cycle length.
2. **Conception Date Mode**: Calculates EDD based on the known date of ovulation or conception.
3. **IVF Embryo Transfer Mode**: Calculates EDD based on a 3-day or 5-day IVF transfer date.

---

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Medical Disclaimer**: This calculator provides educational estimations based on standard clinical algorithms. It does not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis, or clinical ultrasound assessments. Always consult your obstetrician, midwife, or healthcare provider for personalized prenatal care.

---

### Understanding the 3 Calculation Modes

#### 1. Last Menstrual Period (LMP) Mode (Naegele's Rule)
Most pregnancies are dated from the **first day of your last menstrual period (LMP)** because most women know when their period started, whereas exact ovulation is harder to pinpoint.
- **Naegele's Formula**:
  $$\text{EDD} = \text{LMP Date} + 280 \text{ days} + (\text{Cycle Length} - 28 \text{ days})$$
- **Cycle Length Adjustment**: Standard Naegele's Rule assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. If your average cycle is 32 days, ovulation occurred approximately 4 days later, so 4 days are added to your due date.

#### 2. Conception Date Mode
If you know your exact ovulation or conception date (e.g. through ovulation tracking kits):
$$\text{EDD} = \text{Conception Date} + 266 \text{ days}$$
*(Fetal development takes approximately 266 days from actual conception).*

#### 3. IVF Embryo Transfer Mode
In In-Vitro Fertilization (IVF), the exact age of the embryo at transfer is precisely known:
- **5-Day Blastocyst Transfer**:
  $$\text{EDD} = \text{Transfer Date} + 261 \text{ days}$$
- **3-Day Cleavage Transfer**:
  $$\text{EDD} = \text{Transfer Date} + 263 \text{ days}$$

---

### Gestational Age vs. Fetal Age

- **Gestational Age**: Measured in weeks and days from the **first day of your LMP**. When your doctor says you are "8 weeks pregnant," the embryo has actually been developing for about 6 weeks.
- **Fetal Age**: The actual age of the developing fetus, measured from **conception** (2 weeks less than gestational age).

---

### Trimester Breakdown

Pregnancy is divided into 3 distinct trimesters:
* **First Trimester**: Weeks 1 through 12 (early development and organ formation).
* **Second Trimester**: Weeks 13 through 26 (rapid growth and baby movements).
* **Third Trimester**: Weeks 27 through 40 (final maturation and preparation for birth).

---

### Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example (Verified against Code)

Suppose the first day of your LMP was **January 1, 2026** (`2026-01-01`) with a standard **28-day cycle**:

1. **Apply Naegele's Rule**:
   $$\text{EDD} = \text{Jan 1, 2026} + 280 \text{ days} + (28 - 28) = \text{Jan 1, 2026} + 280 \text{ days}$$
2. **Calculate Calendar Target**:
   - January has 30 remaining days ($31 - 1 = 30$).
   - Adding 280 days to Jan 1, 2026 reaches **October 8, 2026**.
3. **Result**: Estimated Due Date (EDD) = **Thu Oct 08 2026**.

---

### Why Due Dates Can Change

Only about **4% of babies are born on their exact estimated due date**. Most healthy deliveries occur between 37 weeks and 42 weeks.

Your healthcare provider may adjust your official due date after performing a **first-trimester dating ultrasound**, which measures the baby's crown-rump length (CRL) for high precision.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: What does an Estimated Due Date (EDD) mean?**
  * A1: An Estimated Due Date (EDD) is a medical estimate marking 40 weeks (280 days) from the first day of your last menstrual period. Only about 4% of babies are born on their exact due date; most arrive within 2 weeks before or after.
* **Q2: What is the difference between Gestational Age and Fetal Age?**
  * A2: Gestational age measures pregnancy progress starting from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP). Fetal age measures actual developmental age starting from conception (typically 2 weeks after LMP).
* **Q3: How are pregnancy trimesters divided?**
  * A3: The First Trimester spans from week 1 to the end of week 12. The Second Trimester spans from week 13 to the end of week 26. The Third Trimester spans from week 27 until delivery.
