# Drip Faucet Calculator

Enter how fast a faucet is dripping, how many faucets are affected, and your local water rate, and this calculator estimates the water — and money — being wasted.

## Formula

**Daily volume (mL) = Drips per minute × Faucets × 0.25 mL × 1,440 minutes**, using the standard plumbing estimate of about 0.25 milliliters per drop. That daily volume converts to liters and gallons, and scales up to monthly (×30.4 days) and annual (×365 days) totals. Multiplying the wasted gallons by your entered water rate estimates the dollar cost.

For a single faucet dripping 20 times a minute at $0.01 per gallon, that works out to about 7.2 liters (1.9 gallons) a day — roughly 58 gallons and $0.58 a month.

## Why the number adds up faster than it seems

A drip rate that sounds trivial second-to-second compounds over a full day's 1,440 minutes. Twenty drips a minute is barely noticeable when you're watching the faucet, but it turns into nearly 2 gallons of water gone by the end of the day, and close to 700 gallons over a year from just that one faucet.

## The dollar cost isn't always the main argument

At typical residential water rates, the direct cost of a single slow drip is often just a few dollars a year — modest on its own. The stronger case for fixing it is usually the sheer volume of clean water wasted, plus the fact that a worsening drip often signals a worn washer or valve that's likely to fail in a more disruptive way eventually. Multiple leaking faucets around a house also add up: this calculator's per-faucet multiplier makes it easy to see how three modest leaks combine into a meaningfully larger total.

## What this doesn't include

This calculates the water cost only, using whatever flat rate you enter. Many actual water bills also add a separate sewage or wastewater charge on top of the water rate itself — sometimes roughly doubling the effective cost per gallon — so check your real bill and build that combined rate into what you enter here if you want a more accurate cost estimate.
