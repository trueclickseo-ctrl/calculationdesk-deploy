# BMI Calculator – Body Mass Index Screening & Categories

**Body Mass Index (BMI)** is a widely recognized mathematical screening ratio that compares an individual's body weight to their height. Developed in the 19th century by Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet, BMI serves as a population-level tool for identifying potential weight categories.

This calculator computes your **BMI score** in both **Metric** and **Imperial** units, identifies your adult screening category, calculates your **healthy weight range for height**, and estimates your **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)** using the clinical **Mifflin-St Jeor equation**.

---

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Health Disclaimer**: BMI is a general statistical screening tool, not a medical diagnosis. It does not measure body composition, muscle density, bone mass, or metabolic health. Do not use BMI as a sole indicator of health status. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional for medical assessments.

---

### The Mathematical Formula for BMI

BMI is calculated by dividing body weight by the square of body height.

#### 1. Metric Formula (Kilograms and Meters)
BMI = frac(Weight (kg)){[Height (m)]^2}
*(If height is in centimeters, divide by 100 first to get meters).*

#### 2. Imperial Formula (Pounds and Inches)
BMI = 703 × frac(Weight (lbs)){[Height (inches)]^2}

---

### Adult Screening Categories

The World Health Organization (WHO) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) define standard adult screening ranges as follows:

| BMI Range | Screening Classification |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Below 18.5** | Falls within the **Underweight** category |
| **18.5 – 24.9** | Falls within the **Normal weight** category |
| **25.0 – 29.9** | Falls within the **Overweight** category |
| **30.0 or Higher** | Falls within the **Obese** category |

*Note: These ranges apply to adults aged 20 and older. Children and teenagers (ages 2–19) are evaluated using age-and-sex-specific BMI percentiles.*

### Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example (Verified against Code)

Let's calculate the BMI for a **25-year-old male** with the following measurements:
- **Height**: 175 cm ($1.75 meters)
- **Weight**: 70 kg

#### 1. Calculate Height Squared
Height^2 = 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625 m^2

#### 2. Calculate BMI Score
BMI = (70 / 3.0625) = 22.857 ≈ 22.9

#### 3. Evaluate Screening Category
A score of 22.9$ falls within the **Normal weight** range ($18.5 to  24.9$).

#### 4. Calculate Healthy Weight Boundaries for 1.75 m
- **Min Healthy Weight ($18.5$)**: 18.5 × 3.0625 = 56.7 kg.
- **Max Healthy Weight ($24.9$)**: 24.9 × 3.0625 = 76.3 kg.

#### 5. Calculate BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor Equation)
BMR = 10(70) + 6.25(175) - 5(25) + 5 = 700 + 1093.75 - 125 + 5 = 1,674 kcal/day

### Why BMI Can Be Misleading (Limitations)

While BMI is helpful for large-scale demographic studies, it has significant limitations for individual health evaluation:

1. **Does Not Distinguish Muscle from Fat**: Athletes, bodybuilders, and active individuals with high muscle mass weigh more per unit volume. As a result, BMI may categorize a lean athlete as "overweight" or "obese."
2. **Ignores Fat Distribution**: Visceral fat (stored around abdominal organs) poses greater health risks than subcutaneous fat (stored under the skin). BMI does not account for waist circumference or fat distribution.
3. **Age & Sarcopenia**: Older adults naturally lose muscle mass (sarcopenia). An elderly individual might have a "normal" BMI score despite having high body fat and low muscle strength.

---

### When BMI Should Not Be Interpreted Alone

BMI should never be used as a standalone health metric for:
- Athletes and heavy resistance trainers
- Pregnant or lactating women
- Children and adolescents (who require growth chart percentiles)
- Elderly individuals experiencing muscle loss
- Individuals with chronic medical conditions affecting fluid balance or muscle composition

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: Is BMI a diagnostic tool for personal health?**
  * A1: No. BMI is an indirect statistical screening ratio that compares weight to height. It does not measure body fat directly, nor does it diagnose illness, cardiovascular risk, or metabolic health.
* **Q2: Why might BMI classify an athlete as overweight?**
  * A2: BMI cannot distinguish between muscle mass and fat mass. Dense muscle weighs more than fat per unit volume, causing muscular individuals to have a high BMI despite having very low body fat.
* **Q3: How does adult BMI differ from child and teenager BMI?**
  * A3: Adult BMI categories use fixed cutoffs (e.g. 18.5 to 24.9). Children and teenagers (ages 2 to 19) must be evaluated using BMI-for-age percentiles that account for ongoing growth and developmental changes.
