### Speed: Distance Over Time

Speed measures how quickly distance is covered. At its core, any speed unit is a ratio of distance to time: meters per second, kilometers per hour, miles per hour. Converting between speed units means adjusting both the distance part and the time part to match the new unit.

This converter handles five speed units: meters per second (m/s), kilometers per hour (km/h), miles per hour (mph), knots (kn), and Mach. Each has its own domain — international roads, aviation, sailing, or scientific measurement.

### How the Conversion Works

The calculator uses meters per second as its internal reference. Every speed unit has a factor expressing how many m/s it equals:

- m/s: 1
- km/h: 0.27778 (= 1/3.6)
- mph: 0.44704
- knot: 0.51444
- Mach: 343 (at sea level, 20°C)

To convert 60 mph to km/h: 60 x 0.44704 = 26.8224 m/s, then 26.8224 / 0.27778 = 96.5608 km/h. This matches exactly the default example shown by the calculator.

### Key Conversion Reference

- 1 mph = 1.609344 km/h (exact)
- 1 km/h = 0.621371 mph
- 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h
- 1 knot = 1.852 km/h (exact)
- 1 knot = 1.15078 mph
- Mach 1 = 343 m/s = 1,234.8 km/h = 767.3 mph (at sea level, 20°C)

### Mach: Speed as a Ratio, Not a Fixed Unit

Mach is fundamentally different from the other units here. It is not a fixed quantity in absolute terms — it is the ratio of an object's speed to the speed of sound in the surrounding medium. The speed of sound changes with temperature: warmer air means sound travels faster, so Mach 1 in hot weather is faster than Mach 1 in cold weather.

At standard sea level conditions (20°C, dry air): Mach 1 = 343 m/s = 1,234.8 km/h.
At cruise altitude for commercial aircraft (approximately -55°C): Mach 1 ≈ 295 m/s = 1,062 km/h.

This converter uses the sea-level standard of 343 m/s. It's the most commonly cited reference and appropriate for general comparisons. For precise aeronautical calculations, the actual altitude and temperature should be used. When a pilot says their aircraft is flying at Mach 0.85, the actual speed in km/h depends on where in the atmosphere they are.

### Practical Contexts

**Driving:** A European speed limit of 130 km/h equals 80.8 mph. The US highway limit of 70 mph is 112.7 km/h. Knowing the rough conversion (multiply km/h by 0.62 for mph, or multiply mph by 1.6 for km/h) helps when driving abroad.

**Running and cycling:** A runner completing a 5K in 25 minutes averages 12 km/h (7.46 mph). A competitive cyclist doing 40 km/h is moving at 24.9 mph.

**Aviation:** Commercial aircraft cruise at roughly 800-920 km/h (Mach 0.78-0.85). Fighter jets reach Mach 2+ (over 2,400 km/h at sea level).

**Maritime:** Ocean-going ships cruise at 15-25 knots (27.8-46.3 km/h). A knot is always slightly faster than a km/h, which is a common source of confusion.
