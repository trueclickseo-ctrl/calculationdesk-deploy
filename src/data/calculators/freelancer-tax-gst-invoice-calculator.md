# Freelancer Tax & GST Invoice Calculator – Complete Financial Flow Guide

Managing finances as an independent contractor or freelancer requires tracking multiple tax components: **gross invoice value, indirect taxes (GST/VAT), tax withholding (TDS), business expense deductions, and net personal income tax**.

This calculator models the exact financial flow from client invoicing to your net take-home earnings.

---

### Understanding the 5 Key Financial Components

1. **Base Invoice Amount**: The agreed-upon pre-tax fee for your professional services.
2. **GST / VAT Amount (Indirect Tax)**: Tax added to the base invoice collected from the client. GST is not profit; it is remitted to tax authorities.
3. **TDS Amount (Tax Deducted at Source)**: Advance tax withheld by the client from your payment. TDS is an asset credit applied toward your annual tax bill.
4. **Business Expenses**: Deductible costs (software, equipment, internet) incurred to deliver services, directly reducing your taxable income.
5. **Estimated Income Tax**: Direct tax levied on your net business profit (Base Invoice - Expenses) based on your personal tax bracket.

---

### Step-by-Step Mathematical Calculation Flow

#### Step 1: Invoice & Client Payout
GST Amount = Base Invoice × frac(GST Rate){100}

Gross Invoice Amount = Base Invoice + GST Amount

TDS Amount = Base Invoice × frac(TDS Rate){100}

Net Client Payout = Gross Invoice Amount - TDS Amount

#### Step 2: Business Profit & Income Tax
Taxable Business Profit = max(0, Base Invoice - Business Expenses)

Income Tax Liability = Taxable Business Profit × frac(Tax Bracket){100}

#### Step 3: Net Take-Home Earnings
Net Take-Home = Taxable Business Profit - Income Tax Liability

### Worked Numerical Example (Verified against Code)

Suppose you issue a **$10,000 Base Invoice** with **18% GST**, **10% TDS**, **$2,000 Business Expenses**, and a **20% Income Tax Bracket**:

1. **Base Invoice**: **$10,000**
2. **GST (18%)**: \10,000 × 0.18 = $1,800
3. **Gross Invoice**: \10,000 + $1,800 = $11,800
4. **TDS Withheld (10%)**: \10,000 × 0.10 = $1,000
5. **Net Payout from Client**: \11,800 - $1,000 = $10,800
6. **Business Expenses**: **$2,000**
7. **Taxable Business Profit**: \10,000 - $2,000 = $8,000
8. **Income Tax Liability (20%)**: \8,000 × 0.20 = $1,600
9. **Net Take-Home Earnings**: \8,000 - $1,600 = $6,400

*Cash Flow Summary*: You receive **$10,800** from your client. You set aside **$1,800** for GST and **$1,600** for Income Tax. After paying **$2,000** in business expenses, your net personal take-home is **$6,400** (with **$1,000 TDS** sitting as a tax credit with the government).

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: Can I claim a refund if TDS withheld exceeds my final income tax?**
  * A1: Yes. If total TDS withheld by clients ($1,000 in example) is greater than your actual income tax due ($1,600 in example, leaving 600 tax due), filing your tax return will reconcile the tax asset. If TDS exceeds tax due, the government refunds the difference.

* **Q2: Does business expense reduce GST payable?**
  * A2: In many jurisdictions, Input Tax Credit (ITC) allows you to offset GST paid on business expenses against GST collected from clients.
