# Momentum Calculator

Enter an object's mass and velocity, and this calculator returns its linear momentum.

## Formula

**Momentum (p) = Mass (m) × Velocity (v)**

For a 10 kg object moving at 5 m/s: p = 10 × 5 = 50 kg·m/s.

## Why momentum is central to collision physics

Momentum is conserved in any closed system where no external force acts — the total momentum of all objects involved in a collision stays the same before and after, whether the collision is a gentle bump or a head-on crash. That conservation principle is what makes momentum such a powerful tool for analyzing collisions: even without knowing the details of the forces involved during impact, tracking momentum before and after tells you a great deal about what happened.

## Momentum versus kinetic energy

It's easy to conflate momentum with kinetic energy, but they follow different rules. Momentum scales linearly with velocity, while kinetic energy scales with the square of velocity — so doubling an object's speed only doubles its momentum but quadruples its kinetic energy. This distinction matters in practice: momentum is what's conserved in every collision, while kinetic energy is only conserved in perfectly elastic ones, with the difference showing up as heat, sound, or deformation in anything less than perfectly elastic.
