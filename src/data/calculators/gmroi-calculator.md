# GMROI Calculator

Enter a gross margin figure and the average inventory cost used to generate it, and this calculator returns the GMROI ratio.

## Formula

**GMROI = Gross Margin ($) ÷ Average Inventory Cost ($)**

For a $60,000 gross margin on $30,000 of average inventory: GMROI = 60,000 ÷ 30,000 = 2.0.

## Why GMROI captures something margin percentage misses

Gross margin percentage alone tells you how profitable a sale is, but it says nothing about how much capital was tied up in inventory to make that sale happen. GMROI folds both factors together — margin and inventory investment — which is why a thin-margin, fast-turning product (think groceries) can post a GMROI just as strong as a high-margin, slow-turning one (think jewelry). It's a measure of how efficiently inventory capital is being converted into profit, not just how profitable each individual sale is.

## What a GMROI score doesn't tell you on its own

A GMROI of 1.0 marks the point where gross margin just covers the average cost of inventory — anything below that means the inventory investment isn't even breaking even before other operating costs like rent and labor enter the picture. But GMROI alone can't tell you whether a low score comes from thin margins, slow-moving inventory, or both — that usually requires looking at margin percentage and inventory turnover separately to diagnose the actual problem.
