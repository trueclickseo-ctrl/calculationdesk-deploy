# Molar Mass Calculator

Enter the number of hydrogen, oxygen, and carbon atoms in a compound's formula, and this calculator computes the resulting molar mass.

## Formula

**Molar Mass = (Hydrogen count × 1.008) + (Oxygen count × 15.999) + (Carbon count × 12.011)**.

For water (H2O), with 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom: molar mass = (2 × 1.008) + (1 × 15.999) = 2.016 + 15.999 = 18.015 g/mol.

## Why molar mass isn't a round number

The atomic mass values used here (1.008 for hydrogen, 15.999 for oxygen, 12.011 for carbon) are themselves isotope-weighted averages, reflecting each element's naturally occurring mix of isotopes rather than the mass of a single "typical" atom. That's why water's molar mass comes out to 18.015 rather than a clean 18 — a small but real distinction that matters for precise stoichiometry work.

## What molar mass connects

Molar mass is the bridge between a substance's mass — something directly measurable on a scale — and the number of moles it represents, a count of actual molecules or formula units. This conversion underlies virtually every stoichiometric calculation in chemistry, from figuring out reaction yields to preparing a solution at a specific target concentration.

## What this calculator doesn't cover

This tool sums atomic masses for hydrogen, oxygen, and carbon specifically. A compound containing any other element — nitrogen, sulfur, a metal, or otherwise — would need that element's atomic mass added to the total separately using its periodic table value, since this calculator's three-element scope doesn't extend to the full periodic table.
