# Centrifuge Force Calculator

Enter a centrifuge's rotor radius and rotational speed, and this calculator returns the relative centrifugal force (RCF) as a multiple of standard gravity.

## Formula

**RCF (× g) = 1.118 × 10⁻⁵ × Radius (mm) × RPM²**

For a rotor with a 100mm radius spinning at 3,000 RPM: RCF = 1.118×10⁻⁵ × 100 × 3,000² ≈ 10,062 × g.

## Why RPM squared matters so much

Because RPM appears squared in the formula, small adjustments to spin speed have an outsized effect on the resulting g-force — doubling the RPM quadruples the RCF, not just doubles it. This is why lab protocols are so precise about the target g-force rather than leaving RPM to rough approximation: a seemingly modest speed change can meaningfully over- or under-force a sensitive sample.

## Why protocols specify RCF instead of RPM

RCF captures the actual physical force experienced by a sample, and it stays consistent across different centrifuges and rotor sizes — a protocol written as "centrifuge at 10,000 x g" means the same thing no matter what equipment is used. An RPM figure alone doesn't, since the same RPM setting produces very different forces on rotors of different radii. Converting between the two, using the specific rotor's actual radius, is a routine and necessary step whenever following a published lab protocol on unfamiliar equipment.
