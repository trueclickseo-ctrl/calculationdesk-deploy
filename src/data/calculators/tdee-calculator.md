# TDEE Calculator – Total Daily Energy Expenditure

Whether your goal is to lose fat, build muscle, or maintain your current physique, everything begins with understanding your **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**.

TDEE is the estimated total number of calories your body burns in a 24-hour day. It accounts for your resting survival metabolism, daily non-exercise movement, physical workouts, and the energy required to digest food.

This calculator computes your TDEE using the **Mifflin-St Jeor formula** for **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)**, multiplied by your **activity level**, and breaks down the 3 primary energy components: **BMR**, **Thermic Effect of Food (TEF)**, and **Physical Activity (PA)**.

---

### BMR vs. TDEE: What Is the Difference?

* **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)**: The calories your body burns while completely at rest (e.g., sleeping in bed for 24 hours). It fuels involuntary vital functions such as pumping blood, breathing, brain activity, and cellular repair. BMR accounts for roughly **60% to 70%** of your daily energy burn.
* **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**: The total energy burned when you add physical movement, exercise, and digestion on top of your BMR.

---

### How TDEE Is Calculated

The calculation works in 3 distinct steps:

#### 1. Basal Metabolic Rate (Mifflin-St Jeor Formula)
The calculator computes your resting baseline using weight ($W$ in kg), height ($H$ in cm), and age ($A$ in years):
- **Men**: $\text{BMR} = 10W + 6.25H - 5A + 5$
- **Women**: $\text{BMR} = 10W + 6.25H - 5A - 161$

#### 2. Applying Activity Multipliers
Your BMR is scaled according to your typical physical activity routine:

| Activity Level | Multiplier | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sedentary** | $1.200$ | Desk job, little or no structured exercise |
| **Lightly Active** | $1.375$ | Light exercise or sports 1–3 days/week |
| **Moderately Active** | $1.550$ | Moderate exercise or sports 3–5 days/week |
| **Very Active** | $1.725$ | Hard exercise or sports 6–7 days/week |
| **Extra Active** | $1.900$ | Very hard daily exercise or heavy physical manual job |

$$\text{TDEE} = \text{BMR} \times \text{Activity Multiplier}$$

#### 3. Metabolic Component Breakdown
* **BMR (Basal Metabolism)**: $\text{BMR Value}$
* **TEF (Thermic Effect of Food)**: Estimated at $10\%$ of TDEE ($\text{TDEE} \times 0.10$), representing the metabolic cost of chewing, digesting, and absorbing nutrients.
* **PA (Physical Activity)**: The remaining energy expended during movement ($\text{PA} = \text{TDEE} - \text{BMR} - \text{TEF}$).

---

### Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example (Verified against Code)

Let's calculate the TDEE for a **25-year-old male**, weighing **70 kg**, height **175 cm**, with a **Moderately Active** routine ($1.55$ multiplier):

1. **Calculate BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor)**:
   $$\text{BMR} = 10(70) + 6.25(175) - 5(25) + 5$$
   $$\text{BMR} = 700 + 1,093.75 - 125 + 5 = \mathbf{1,673.75 \text{ kcal/day}} \quad (\approx 1,674 \text{ kcal})$$
2. **Calculate TDEE**:
   $$\text{TDEE} = 1,673.75 \times 1.55 = \mathbf{2,594.31 \text{ kcal/day}} \quad (\approx 2,594 \text{ kcal})$$
3. **Calculate Thermic Effect of Food (TEF)**:
   $$\text{TEF} = 2,594.31 \times 0.10 = \mathbf{259.43 \text{ kcal}} \quad (\approx 259 \text{ kcal})$$
4. **Calculate Physical Activity Expenditure (PA)**:
   $$\text{PA} = 2,594 - 1,674 - 259 = \mathbf{661 \text{ kcal}}$$

---

### Using TDEE for Body Weight Management

Once you know your TDEE baseline, you can set target calorie budgets:
* **Weight Maintenance**: Consume your calculated TDEE calories daily.
* **Fat Loss Deficit**: Subtract $500 \text{ kcal/day}$ from TDEE ($\approx 1 \text{ lb}$ fat loss per week).
* **Muscle Gain Surplus**: Add $250 \text{ to } 500 \text{ kcal/day}$ to TDEE to support lean tissue synthesis.

---

### Real-World Adjustments & Limitations

TDEE calculators provide a **starting benchmark**, not a rigid prescription. Actual metabolism varies due to:
* **Body Composition**: Muscle tissue burns more resting energy than fat mass.
* **NEAT (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis)**: Unconscious fidgeting, walking, and posture adjustments vary widely between individuals.
* **How to Adjust**: Track your body weight average over 2–3 weeks. If you are eating at your calculated TDEE but gaining weight, reduce daily calories by $150\text{--}200 \text{ kcal}$. If losing weight unexpectedly, increase intake.

---

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: Is TDEE the same as maintenance calories?**
  * A1: Yes. TDEE represents your baseline maintenance calories. Eating at your TDEE keeps your body weight stable. Eating 500 calories below TDEE creates a fat loss deficit, while eating 500 calories above TDEE creates a muscle gain surplus.
* **Q2: Should I eat my exact TDEE number every day?**
  * A2: No. Human metabolism and activity fluctuate daily. TDEE is a guide for your average daily intake over a week rather than an exact daily rule.
* **Q3: Why might my real-world maintenance calories differ from the calculator?**
  * A3: Population formulas cannot measure individual muscle mass, thyroid hormone levels, digestive efficiency, or subtle fidgeting movement (NEAT). Use the calculator output as a starting benchmark and adjust based on weekly scale trends.
