# FD Calculator – Fixed Deposit Interest & Maturity Payout Guide

A **Fixed Deposit (FD)** is a safe financial term deposit offered by commercial banks and financial companies. FDs allow investors to lock in a guaranteed interest rate for a specific tenure, earning higher interest than standard savings accounts.

---

### How Fixed Deposits Work

When opening a Fixed Deposit:
1. You invest a **lump-sum principal amount** for a selected time period (e.g., 1 to 10 years).
2. The bank guarantees a **fixed annual interest rate** for the entire tenure.
3. Interest is **compounded periodically** (typically quarterly, monthly, half-yearly, or yearly) and paid out along with your principal at maturity.

#### The Mathematical FD Compound Interest Formula

$$A = P \times \left(1 + \frac{r}{n \times 100}\right)^{n \times t}$$

Where:
- **$A$**: Total maturity amount payout.
- **$P$**: Principal investment deposit amount.
- **$r$**: Annual interest rate (percentage).
- **$n$**: Number of compounding periods per year ($12$ for monthly, $4$ for quarterly, $2$ for half-yearly, $1$ for yearly).
- **$t$**: Investment tenure in years.

---

### Worked FD Example with Actual Numbers

Let's calculate the maturity value of a **$100,000 principal investment** at a **6.5% annual interest rate** compounded **quarterly ($n = 4$)** over **5 years ($t = 5$)**:

1. **Identify Variables**:
   $$P = 100,000, \quad r = 6.5, \quad n = 4, \quad t = 5$$
2. **Calculate Quarterly Interest Rate**:
   $$\text{Rate per quarter} = \frac{6.5}{4 \times 100} = 0.01625$$
3. **Calculate Total Compounding Periods**:
   $$\text{Total Periods} = 4 \times 5 = 20\text{ quarters}$$
4. **Compute Maturity Amount ($A$)**:
   $$A = 100,000 \times (1 + 0.01625)^{20}$$
   $$A = 100,000 \times (1.01625)^{20} = 100,000 \times 1.38042 \approx \$138,042$$
5. **Summary**:
   - **Invested Principal**: **$100,000**
   - **Total Interest Earned**: $\$138,042 - \$100,000 = \mathbf{\$38,042}$
   - **Total Maturity Value**: **$138,042**

---

### Impact of Compounding Frequency & Tenure

- **Compounding Frequency Effect**: Compounding more frequently increases total returns because earned interest generates further interest faster.
  - *Yearly Compounding ($n = 1$)*: $100,000 \times (1.065)^5 = \$137,009$
  - *Quarterly Compounding ($n = 4$)*: $100,000 \times (1.01625)^{20} = \$138,042$
  - *Monthly Compounding ($n = 12$)*: $100,000 \times (1 + 6.5/1200)^{60} = \$138,282$
- **Tenure Effect**: Longer tenures maximize the exponential curve of compound interest, providing substantial wealth protection against market volatility.

---

### Tax Considerations & Limitations

1. **Tax Deducted at Source (TDS)**: Fixed deposit interest is fully taxable. Banks deduct TDS (typically 10%) if total annual interest across all term deposits exceeds regulatory thresholds (e.g., ₹40,000 in India).
2. **Premature Penalty**: Withdrawing an FD prior to maturity usually incurs a penalty charge (0.5%–1.0% reduced interest rate).
