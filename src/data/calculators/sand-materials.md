# Sand Calculator

Enter the length, width, and desired depth of a project area, and this calculator returns the volume and weight of sand needed.

## Formula

**Cubic Yards = (Length × Width × (Depth in inches ÷ 12)) ÷ 27**, then **Tons = Cubic Yards × 1.35**

For a bed 20 ft long, 15 ft wide, and 2 inches deep: Yards = (20 × 15 × (2/12)) ÷ 27 ≈ 1.85; Tons = 1.85 × 1.35 = 2.5.

## Matching depth to the actual job

The right depth to enter depends heavily on what the sand is actually for. Paver bedding calls for a thin, precise layer — typically just 1 to 1.5 inches after compaction — since its job is leveling and allowing pavers to settle tightly, not bulk fill. A sandbox or larger fill application, by contrast, might call for several inches or more. Using a paving-appropriate depth for a fill project (or vice versa) will produce a materially wrong quantity estimate even though the formula itself is correct.

## Why wet sand throws off the standard conversion

The 1.35 tons-per-cubic-yard figure this calculator uses applies to dry sand. Because sand is porous, water fills the spaces between grains without meaningfully increasing volume, so sand that's absorbed rain or been stored uncovered can weigh 15-25% more per cubic yard than the dry-condition estimate suggests. If there's any chance the delivered material will be wet, it's worth padding the tonnage estimate accordingly or confirming actual density with the supplier.
