# Calorie Calculator – Daily Energy Expenditure & Targets

Managing body weight — whether the goal is weight maintenance, gradual fat loss, or muscle gain — starts with understanding how many calories the body actually burns in a day. That number is not just the calories spent during a workout; it includes resting metabolism combined with all the movement and digestion that happens throughout a normal day.

This calculator computes Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, multiplies it by an activity-level factor to estimate Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE), and then converts that into daily calorie targets for maintaining, losing, or gaining weight.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Health disclaimer**: This calculator provides estimates based on population averages. It cannot guarantee a specific weight loss or weight gain outcome. Avoid extreme calorie restriction, and consult a physician or registered dietitian before starting a new diet or exercise program.

## Understanding the Key Concepts: BMR vs. TDEE

**Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)** is the baseline energy the body burns at complete rest just to stay alive — heartbeat, brain activity, and organ maintenance. **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)** is the total number of calories burned in 24 hours, combining BMR with physical activity and the energy used to digest food.

$$\text{TDEE} = \text{BMR} \times \text{Activity Multiplier}$$

## Physical Activity Level Multipliers

The calculator applies one of five standard activity multipliers to BMR:

| Activity Level | Description | Multiplier |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sedentary** | Little to no structured exercise | 1.200 |
| **Lightly Active** | Light exercise 1–3 days per week | 1.375 |
| **Moderately Active** | Moderate exercise 3–5 days per week | 1.550 |
| **Very Active** | Hard exercise 6–7 days per week | 1.725 |
| **Extra Active** | Very hard exercise or a physically demanding job | 1.900 |

## Weight Loss, Maintenance, and Weight Gain Targets

**Weight Maintenance Target.** To hold current weight steady, daily intake should match TDEE: Maintenance Target = TDEE.

**Weight Loss Target (500-calorie deficit).** One pound (about 0.45 kg) of body fat stores roughly 3,500 calories. A daily deficit of 500 calories produces a weekly deficit of about 3,500 calories (500 × 7), supporting gradual fat loss of roughly 0.5 kg per week: Weight Loss Target = TDEE − 500 kcal/day.

**Weight Gain Target (500-calorie surplus).** For gradual weight gain or muscle-building support: Weight Gain Target = TDEE + 500 kcal/day.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Sedentary Female, BMR 1,400 kcal/day

TDEE $= 1{,}400 \times 1.200 = 1{,}680$ kcal/day.

Weight Loss Target $= 1{,}680 - 500 = 1{,}180$ kcal/day.

Weight Gain Target $= 1{,}680 + 500 = 2{,}180$ kcal/day.

### Example 2: Moderately Active Male, 35 yrs, 180 cm, 85 kg

BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor) $= 10(85) + 6.25(180) - 5(35) + 5 = 850 + 1125 - 175 + 5 = 1{,}805$ kcal/day.

TDEE $= 1{,}805 \times 1.550 = 2{,}797.75 \approx 2{,}798$ kcal/day.

Weight Loss Target $= 2{,}798 - 500 = 2{,}298$ kcal/day.

Weight Gain Target $= 2{,}798 + 500 = 3{,}298$ kcal/day.

## Why Real-World Energy Needs Vary

Calculated TDEE is an estimate, and real-world energy expenditure can fluctuate for several reasons. **NEAT (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis)** — fidgeting, posture changes, walking around the house — varies significantly between individuals and isn't captured by the fixed activity multipliers. **Muscle mass** raises resting energy burn even within the same activity bracket, since muscle tissue is more metabolically active than fat. **Adaptive thermogenesis** can also cause the body to slightly lower non-essential energy expenditure during a prolonged calorie deficit, which is one reason weight loss often slows over time even when intake stays constant.

## What This Calculator Does Not Include

> [!NOTE]
> **Real-world exclusions**: This calculator applies fixed activity multipliers and a flat 500-calorie adjustment. It does not account for individual metabolic adaptation, medical conditions, medications, or precise body-composition differences. For a highly individualized calorie target, consult a registered dietitian.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a calorie deficit?

A calorie deficit occurs when daily energy intake from food and drink is less than daily energy expenditure (TDEE), forcing the body to draw on stored body fat for energy.

### Why is a 500-calorie daily deficit commonly used?

One pound of body fat contains roughly 3,500 calories of stored energy. A daily deficit of 500 calories creates a cumulative weekly deficit of about 3,500 calories (500 × 7 = 3,500), supporting gradual weight loss of about 0.5 kg (1 lb) per week.

### What is the minimum safe daily calorie threshold?

Clinical guidelines generally recommend that daily intake not drop below about 1,200 calories per day for women or 1,500 calories per day for men without medical supervision, to help ensure adequate micronutrient intake.

### How is this calculator different from the BMR Calculator?

The [BMR Calculator](/calculators/bmr-calculator/) only estimates resting energy expenditure. This calculator takes that BMR figure a step further by multiplying it by an activity-level factor to produce a full daily calorie target for maintenance, loss, or gain.
