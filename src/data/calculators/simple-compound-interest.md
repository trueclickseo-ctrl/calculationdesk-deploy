# Simple vs Compound Interest Calculator – Return Comparison Guide

Understanding the difference between **Simple Interest** and **Compound Interest** is fundamental to financial literacy.

- **Simple Interest**: Returns grow linearly. Interest is calculated strictly as a percentage of your starting principal.
- **Compound Interest**: Returns grow exponentially. Interest is calculated on your starting principal plus all interest earned in prior periods.

---

### How the Comparison Works

This calculator applies both formulas side-by-side to identical inputs ($P, R, T$), allowing you to select your preferred compounding frequency ($n$).

#### Mathematical Comparison Formulas

#### 1. Simple Interest (SI)
$$\text{SI} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$$

$$\text{Simple Total} = P + \text{SI}$$

#### 2. Compound Interest (CI)
$$\text{Compound Total} = P \times \left(1 + \frac{R}{n \times 100}\right)^{n \times T}$$

$$\text{CI} = \text{Compound Total} - P$$

#### 3. Net Compounding Benefit
$$\text{Compounding Benefit} = \text{Compound Total} - \text{Simple Total}$$

Where:
- **$P$**: Starting principal amount.
- **$R$**: Annual interest rate (%).
- **$T$**: Tenure in years.
- **$n$**: Compounding frequency per year ($n=12$ monthly, $n=4$ quarterly, $n=2$ half-yearly, $n=1$ yearly).

---

### Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example (Verified against Code)

Suppose you invest **$10,000** at an **8% annual rate** for **5 years**, selecting **yearly compounding ($n=1$)**:

#### 1. Simple Interest Calculation:
$$\text{SI} = \frac{10,000 \times 8 \times 5}{100} = \mathbf{\$4,000}$$

$$\text{Simple Total} = \$10,000 + \$4,000 = \mathbf{\$14,000}$$

#### 2. Compound Interest Calculation:
$$\text{Compound Total} = 10,000 \times (1 + 0.08)^5 = 10,000 \times 1.469328 \approx \mathbf{\$14,693.28}$$

$$\text{CI} = \$14,693.28 - \$10,000 = \mathbf{\$4,693.28}$$

#### 3. Net Compounding Benefit:
$$\text{Benefit} = \$14,693.28 - \$14,000.00 = \mathbf{\$693.28}$$

*Takeaway*: Over 5 years, compounding yields an extra **$693** on a $10,000 investment. Over 20 years, that compounding bonus expands to over **$20,500**!

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: Which interest type is better for borrowing a loan?**
  * A1: For borrowing money, simple interest is better because your total repayment liability remains lower. For saving and investing, compound interest is far superior.
