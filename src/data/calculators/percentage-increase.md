# Percentage Increase Calculator

Enter an original value and a percentage, and this calculator returns the final value after that percentage is added on top.

## The formula

**Final = Original × (1 + Increase% / 100)**. A 15% increase on $100 means multiplying by 1.15, giving $115 — the extra $15 is exactly 15% of the starting $100.

## Where people get tripped up: stacking increases

Percentage increases don't add together in a simple way. If a price goes up 10% one year and another 10% the next, the total increase over both years is 21%, not 20% — because the second 10% is calculated on the already-higher price, not the original one. Each increase has to be applied to the running total in sequence: $100 → $110 (first 10%) → $121 (second 10%, applied to $110). This is the same reason compound interest grows faster than simple interest over multiple periods.
