# Water Intake Calculator – Daily Hydration Requirements

Water is essential for life. It makes up roughly **60% of human body weight** and plays a vital role in regulating body temperature, lubricating joints, transporting nutrients, flushing waste products, and supporting cellular function.

However, there is no single universal prescription for daily water consumption. An athlete running in hot weather requires significantly more fluid than a sedentary office worker in an air-conditioned room.

This calculator computes your **recommended daily water intake target** based on your **body weight**, **daily exercise duration**, and **ambient climate temperature**, displaying results in **milliliters (ml)**, **Liters (L)**, **fluid ounces (fl oz)**, and **standard 8-oz cups**.

---

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Hydration Health Disclaimer**: This calculator provides general fluid estimations for healthy individuals. Individuals with kidney disease, heart failure, liver conditions, or those taking fluid-restricting medications should follow specific fluid allowances prescribed by their physician.

---

### How Daily Fluid Needs Are Calculated

The calculator builds your fluid target in 3 steps:

#### 1. Baseline Requirement (Metabolic Mass)
Your baseline water need scales directly with body weight:
- **Metric System**: Base (ml) = Weight (kg) × 35 ml/kg
- **Imperial System**: Base (fl oz) = Weight (lbs) × 0.5 oz/lb

#### 2. Physical Exercise Adjustment
Physical activity increases body heat and sweat rate. The calculator adds fluid for workout duration:
- **Metric**: +350 ml for every 30 minutes of physical workout (frac(Exercise Mins){30} × 350).
- **Imperial**: +12 fl oz for every 30 minutes of physical workout (frac(Exercise Mins){30} × 12).

#### 3. Climate Temperature Adjustment
Hot or humid weather accelerates perspiration and respiratory evaporation:
- **Hot Climate**: Adds +500 ml (+17 fl oz).
- **Moderate / Cold Climate**: No additional adjustment.

---

### Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example (Verified against Code)

Let's calculate the water target for an **80 kg individual** who performs **60 minutes of exercise** in a **hot climate**:

1. **Calculate Baseline Fluid**:
 Base = 80 kg × 35 ml/kg = 2,800 ml
2. **Calculate Exercise Fluid**:
 Exercise = frac(60 mins){30} × 350 ml = 2 × 350 = 700 ml
3. **Calculate Climate Fluid**:
 Hot Climate = 500 ml
4. **Total Daily Fluid Target**:
 Total = 2800 + 700 + 500 = 4,000 ml (4.0 Liters)
5. **Convert to Cups and Fluid Ounces**:
 - Fluid Ounces: 4000 / 29.5735 ≈ 135.3 fl oz.
 - Standard 8-oz Cups: 135.3 / 8 ≈ 16.9 cups (≈ 17 cups)$.

*Verification Result: Total Water = **4,000 ml** (4.0 L / 135 fl oz / ~17 cups).*

---

### Plain Water vs. Total Fluid Intake

It is important to distinguish between **plain drinking water** and **total fluid intake**:
- Approximately **20% of your daily fluid intake** comes naturally from moisture in foods (fruits, vegetables, soups).
- Beverages such as herbal teas, milk, and clear broths also contribute toward your daily fluid balance.
- Pure water remains the ideal primary beverage because it contains zero calories, sugars, or additives.

---

### Practical Tips for Healthy Hydration

* **Monitor Urine Color**: Pale yellow (like lemonade) indicates healthy hydration. Dark amber urine indicates you need to drink more fluids.
* **Drink Proactively During Exercise**: Sip water before, during, and after physical activity rather than waiting until you feel severely thirsty.
* **Listen to Thirst Cues**: Your body's natural thirst mechanism is a reliable guide under normal conditions.

---

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: Is the '8 glasses of water a day' rule accurate for everyone?**
  * A1: No. The '8x8 rule' (8 glasses of 8 ounces, or ~1.9 Liters) is a simple mnemonic, but actual fluid needs vary significantly depending on body size, sweat rate, physical activity, and ambient temperature.
* **Q2: Do beverages like tea, coffee, and water-rich foods count toward fluid intake?**
  * A2: Yes. Caffeinated beverages (in moderate amounts), herbal teas, soups, fruits (like watermelon and oranges), and vegetables contribute toward your total daily fluid intake.
* **Q3: Can drinking too much water be dangerous?**
  * A3: Yes. Consuming extreme amounts of plain water faster than the kidneys can excrete it can lead to hyponatremia (abnormally low blood sodium levels). Listen to your body's thirst cues.
