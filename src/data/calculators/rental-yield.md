# Rental Yield Calculator – Calculate Gross and Net Rental Yields

When evaluating real estate investments, purchase price alone does not indicate whether a property will generate attractive cash returns. Investors use **rental yield** to measure the annual income return generated by a property relative to its acquisition cost.

This calculator computes both **Gross Rental Yield** (top-line revenue return) and **Net Rental Yield** (real return after operating expenses).

---

### Formulas & Calculation Engine

#### 1. Gross Annual Revenue ($R_g$)
$$R_g = \text{Monthly Rent} \times 12$$

#### 2. Net Annual Revenue ($R_n$)
$$R_n = R_g - \text{Annual Operating Expenses}$$

#### 3. Gross Rental Yield ($Y_g$)
$$\mathbf{Y_g = \left( \frac{R_g}{\text{Property Purchase Price}} \right) \times 100}$$

#### 4. Net Rental Yield ($Y_n$)
$$\mathbf{Y_n = \left( \frac{R_n}{\text{Property Purchase Price}} \right) \times 100}$$

### Verified Step-by-Step Worked Example

Let's calculate rental yields for a **$300,000 investment property** generating **$2,000/month in rent** with **$4,000 in annual operating expenses**:

#### Step 1: Calculate Gross Annual Revenue
$$R_g = \$2,000 \times 12 = \mathbf{\$24,000 / year}$$

#### Step 2: Calculate Net Annual Revenue
$$R_n = \$24,000 - \$4,000 = \mathbf{\$20,000 / year}$$

#### Step 3: Compute Gross Rental Yield
$$Y_g = \left( \frac{\$24,000}{\$300,000} \right) \times 100 = 0.08 \times 100 = \mathbf{8.00\%}$$

#### Step 4: Compute Net Rental Yield
$$Y_n = \left( \frac{\$20,000}{\$300,000} \right) \times 100 = 0.06667 \times 100 = \mathbf{6.67\%}$$

### What Belongs in Operating Expenses vs. Excluded Costs

To ensure an accurate Net Yield calculation:

* **INCLUDE in Operating Expenses**: Property taxes, landlord hazard insurance, routine maintenance/repairs, property management fees (typically 8%–10% of rent), HOA/service charges, and landlord-paid utility fees.
* **EXCLUDED from Yield Formula**: Mortgage principal and interest payments, closing acquisition costs, income taxes, vacancy reserves, and major capital expenditures (e.g. roof replacement).

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: Is Rental Yield the same as Return on Investment (ROI)?**
  * A1: No. Rental yield measures ongoing annual income relative to property price. Total ROI includes rental cash flow PLUS capital growth appreciation over time.
* **Q2: Should I include acquisition fees in the purchase price?**
  * A2: Yes. For a precise calculation of yield on total capital outlay, add stamp duty, legal fees, and closing costs to the purchase price denominator.
* **Q3: Why do high-value cities have low rental yields?**
  * A3: In major metropolitan areas, high property prices compress rental yields (often 3% to 4%), but investors accept lower cash yields in anticipation of higher long-term capital appreciation.
