# Rental Yield Calculator – Gross and Net Rental Yields

Evaluating rental property investments based solely on purchase price or monthly rent can be misleading. A property generating $3,000/month may look attractive until high HOA dues, property taxes, and insurance trim away operating profits. Conversely, a modest $1,500/month rental in a low-tax area might yield higher net cash flow.

This calculator computes **Gross Yield (%)**, **Net Yield (%)**, **Gross Annual Revenue**, and **Net Annual Revenue**.

---

### Formulas & Yield Accounting Rules

Rental yields express annual rental income as a percentage of total property purchase price:

#### 1. Gross Annual Yield ($Y_{\text{gross}}$, in %)
Gross yield evaluates top-line rental income relative to purchase price before deducting operational expenses:

$$\text{Gross Annual Revenue} = \text{Monthly Rent} \times 12$$

$$\mathbf{Y_{\text{gross}} = \left( \frac{\text{Gross Annual Revenue}}{\text{Purchase Price}} \right) \times 100}$$

#### 2. Net Annual Yield ($Y_{\text{net}}$, in %)
Net yield deducts all recurring annual operating expenses to measure true bottom-line cash flow performance:

$$\text{Net Annual Revenue} = \text{Gross Annual Revenue} - \text{Annual Operating Expenses}$$

$$\mathbf{Y_{\text{net}} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Annual Revenue}}{\text{Purchase Price}} \right) \times 100}$$

### Verified Step-by-Step Worked Example

Let's calculate rental yields for a property purchased for **$300,000**, renting for **$2,000/month**, with **$4,000 in annual operating expenses**:

#### Step 1: Calculate Gross Annual Revenue
$$\text{Gross Annual Revenue} = \$2,000 \times 12 = \mathbf{\$24,000.00 / year}$$

#### Step 2: Compute Gross Rental Yield
$$\text{Gross Yield} = \left( \frac{\$24,000}{\$300,000} \right) \times 100 = 0.08 \times 100 = \mathbf{8.00\%}$$

#### Step 3: Compute Net Annual Revenue
$$\text{Net Annual Revenue} = \$24,000 - \$4,000 = \mathbf{\$20,000.00 / year}$$

#### Step 4: Compute Net Rental Yield
$$\text{Net Yield} = \left( \frac{\$20,000}{\$300,000} \right) \times 100 = 0.06666 \times 100 = \mathbf{6.67\%}$$

---

### Second Worked Example: Higher-Value Urban Condo

For a **$500,000 condo** renting for **$3,000/month** with **$9,000/year in operating expenses**:

$$\text{Gross Annual Revenue} = \$3{,}000 \times 12 = \mathbf{\$36{,}000.00}$$
$$\text{Gross Yield} = \left( \frac{36{,}000}{500{,}000} \right) \times 100 = \mathbf{7.20\%}$$
$$\text{Net Annual Revenue} = 36{,}000 - 9{,}000 = \mathbf{\$27{,}000.00}$$
$$\text{Net Yield} = \left( \frac{27{,}000}{500{,}000} \right) \times 100 = \mathbf{5.40\%}$$

> [!NOTE]
> Despite a lower Gross Yield than the baseline example (7.20% vs. 8.00%), this condo generates $7,000 more in annual net revenue simply because of its larger absolute rent and purchase price - a reminder that yield percentage and absolute cash flow are two different (and both useful) ways to compare properties.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: What is the difference between Rental Yield and Cash-on-Cash Return?**
  * A1: Rental Yield measures return on the total property price (assuming an all-cash purchase). Cash-on-Cash Return measures net cash flow relative strictly to the down payment and out-of-pocket cash invested when using a mortgage.
* **Q2: Does Net Yield include capital appreciation?**
  * A2: No. Rental yield measures income returns generated by tenant rents. Capital appreciation (property price growth over time) is accounted for in Total Investment Return (IRR).
* **Q3: Why might two properties with the same Net Yield still be very different investments?**
  * A3: Yield percentage alone doesn't capture risk factors like neighborhood appreciation potential, tenant quality, vacancy risk, or the condition of the building - two properties can share an identical net yield today while having very different long-term outlooks.
