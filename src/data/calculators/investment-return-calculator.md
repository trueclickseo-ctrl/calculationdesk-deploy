# Investment Return Calculator – Performance & ROI Guide

Evaluating asset returns requires looking at both **total profit** earned over the entire holding period and **annualized growth** achieved per year.

This calculator computes your **total profit gain, absolute Return on Investment (ROI), Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR), and asset value multiplier**.

---

### How Investment Return Calculations Work

#### 1. Total Profit Gain
$$\text{Total Profit} = \text{Ending Value} + \text{Dividends/Income} - \text{Initial Cost}$$

#### 2. Absolute Return on Investment (ROI %)
$$\text{ROI} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Profit}}{\text{Initial Cost}} \right) \times 100$$

#### 3. Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR %)
$$\text{CAGR} = \left( \left( \frac{\text{Ending Value} + \text{Dividends}}{\text{Initial Cost}} \right)^{\frac{1}{t}} - 1 \right) \times 100$$

#### 4. Value Multiplier
$$\text{Multiplier} = \frac{\text{Ending Value} + \text{Dividends}}{\text{Initial Cost}}$$

Where $t$ is the holding tenure in years.

---

### Verified Step-by-Step Worked Example

Suppose you purchase shares for an initial cost of **$20,000**, hold them for **3 years**, sell them for an ending market value of **$26,000**, and receive **$1,000 in dividends** over the holding period:

#### Step 1: Calculate Total Realized Value
$$\text{Total Realized Value} = \$26,000 + \$1,000 = \mathbf{\$27,000}$$

#### Step 2: Calculate Total Profit Gain
$$\text{Total Profit} = \$27,000 - \$20,000 = \mathbf{\$7,000}$$

#### Step 3: Calculate Absolute Return on Investment (ROI)
$$\text{ROI} = \left( \frac{\$7,000}{\$20,000} \right) \times 100 = \mathbf{35.00\%}$$

#### Step 4: Calculate Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)
$$\text{CAGR} = \left( \left( \frac{\$27,000}{\$20,000} \right)^{\frac{1}{3}} - 1 \right) \times 100 = \left( (1.35)^{0.333} - 1 \right) \times 100 \approx \mathbf{10.52\%}$$

#### Summary
- **Initial Investment**: **$20,000**
- **Ending Value + Dividends**: **$27,000**
- **Total Profit**: **$7,000**
- **Absolute ROI**: **35.00%**
- **Annualized CAGR**: **10.52%**
- **Asset Multiplier**: **x1.35**

---

### Second Worked Example: Longer Holding Period at a Similar ROI

An asset purchased for **$10,000**, held for **5 years**, sold for **$15,000**, plus **$500** in dividends:

$$\text{Total Realized Value} = 15{,}000 + 500 = \mathbf{\$15{,}500}, \quad \text{Total Profit} = 15{,}500 - 10{,}000 = \mathbf{\$5{,}500}$$
$$\text{ROI} = \left( \frac{5{,}500}{10{,}000} \right) \times 100 = \mathbf{55.00\%}$$
$$\text{CAGR} = \left( \left( \frac{15{,}500}{10{,}000} \right)^{\frac{1}{5}} - 1 \right) \times 100 \approx \mathbf{9.16\%}$$

> [!NOTE]
> This example has a higher absolute ROI than the first example (55.00% vs. 35.00%), but a lower annualized CAGR (9.16% vs. 10.52%), because its return was spread across 5 years instead of 3. This is exactly why CAGR, not raw ROI, is the right metric for comparing investments held over different time periods.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: Can ROI be negative?**
  * A1: Yes. If the sum of your ending market value and dividends is less than your initial purchase price, your total profit will be negative, resulting in a negative ROI percentage representing a net capital loss.
* **Q2: Why does CAGR use an exponent instead of simple division by the number of years?**
  * A2: Simple division (ROI ÷ years) ignores compounding and understates true annualized growth for multi-year investments. CAGR's exponent correctly accounts for the fact that each year's growth builds on the previous year's already-grown value, matching how compound growth actually works.
* **Q3: Should I use ROI or CAGR to compare two different investments?**
  * A3: Use CAGR whenever the investments were held for different lengths of time, since it normalizes for holding period. ROI is only a fair comparison metric when both investments were held for the exact same duration.
