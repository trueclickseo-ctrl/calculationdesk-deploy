# Mass Percent Calculator

Enter the mass of a solute and a solvent, and this calculator computes the resulting mass percent concentration.

## Formula

**Mass Percent = (Solute Mass ÷ (Solute Mass + Solvent Mass)) × 100**.

10 grams of solute in 90 grams of solvent gives a mass percent of (10 ÷ 100) × 100 = 10%.

## Total solution mass, not just solvent mass

The denominator in this calculation is the total solution mass — solute plus solvent together — not the solvent alone. This trips people up regularly: dividing 10g of solute by just the 90g of solvent (rather than the full 100g solution) would incorrectly give 11.1% instead of the correct 10%.

## Mass percent vs. molarity

Mass percent is a weight-based ratio, while molarity measures moles of solute per liter of solution — a fundamentally different, volume-based unit. Converting between the two requires knowing both the solute's molar mass and the solution's density, since mass percent alone doesn't specify how much physical space the solution takes up.

## A temperature-stable measurement

Because mass doesn't change with temperature the way liquid volume does through thermal expansion, mass percent stays constant regardless of temperature — unlike molarity, which can shift slightly as a solution's volume changes with heat. This makes mass percent a more stable choice for precise formulations where temperature might vary during preparation or storage.
