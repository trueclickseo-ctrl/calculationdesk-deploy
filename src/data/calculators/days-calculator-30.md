# 30 Day Calculator

Enter a starting date, and this calculator instantly finds the exact calendar date 30 days after and 30 days before it.

## Formula

**Future date = Starting date + 30 days**. **Past date = Starting date − 30 days**. The calculation works from actual calendar date objects, so it automatically handles month-length differences and leap years correctly.

For January 1, 2026: 30 days in the future is January 31, 2026, and 30 days in the past is December 2, 2025.

## Why 30 days isn't "next month, same date"

Calendar months range from 28 to 31 days, so a 30-day offset doesn't reliably land on the same numbered day of the following month. From January 1 (a 31-day month), adding 30 days lands on January 31 — still in January — rather than February 1. This is a common point of confusion when people assume "30 days" means "one month."

## Net-30 and other common 30-day windows

A 30-day offset shows up constantly in everyday deadlines: net-30 invoice payment terms (calendar days, not business days, unless a contract says otherwise), many retail return and refund windows, software free-trial periods, and some security deposit return deadlines. Because the calculation is based on real date objects, it stays accurate whether the starting date falls near a month boundary, a year boundary, or February in a leap year.
