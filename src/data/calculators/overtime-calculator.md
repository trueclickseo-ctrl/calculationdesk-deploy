# Overtime Calculator – Regular, Overtime & Double-Time Pay

When hourly employees work extra hours beyond their standard weekly schedule, labor laws and employment contracts often entitle them to **premium pay rates**.

Understanding how your timesheet translates into your gross paycheck requires calculating three distinct pay categories: **Regular Pay**, **Standard Overtime Pay (Time-and-a-Half / 1.5x)**, and **Double-Time Pay (2.0x)**.

This calculator computes your **Gross Paycheck ($)**, **Total Work Hours**, **Average Hourly Rate ($/hr)**, **Regular Earnings ($)**, **Overtime Earnings ($)**, and **Double-Time Earnings ($)**.

---

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Legal Disclaimer**: This calculator computes mathematical earnings based on user-entered hours and multipliers. It does not determine legal overtime entitlement. Overtime rules vary significantly by country, state (e.g. California daily overtime rules), union contracts, and worker exemption classifications under laws such as the US Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA).

---

### Understanding Wage Categories

* **Regular Hours**: The baseline standard work hours in a pay period (typically up to 40 hours per week in the US).
* **Regular Hourly Rate (R)**: Your agreed base pay rate per standard hour worked.
* **Overtime Multiplier (M)**: The premium factor applied to overtime hours (standard FLSA rate is 1.5×, or "time-and-a-half," though this field can be set to any value).
* **Overtime Rate**: The elevated hourly rate for overtime hours (R × M).
* **Double-Time Rate**: The premium rate equal to twice your regular rate (R × 2.0), tracked in its own separate hours field.
* **Gross Paycheck**: Total pre-tax compensation earned across all work hours.
* **Average Hourly Rate**: Your effective hourly earnings across the entire pay period (Gross Pay ÷ Total Hours).

---

### Overtime Calculation Formulas

The calculator processes your timesheet in three sequential steps:

#### Step 1: Calculate Regular Pay

$$\text{Regular Pay} = \text{Regular Hours} \times \text{Regular Rate (R)}$$

#### Step 2: Calculate Overtime & Double-Time Pay

$$\text{Overtime Rate} = R \times \text{OT Multiplier (M)} \qquad \text{Overtime Pay} = \text{Overtime Hours} \times \text{Overtime Rate}$$

$$\text{Double-Time Rate} = R \times 2 \qquad \text{Double-Time Pay} = \text{Double Hours} \times \text{Double-Time Rate}$$

#### Step 3: Calculate Gross Paycheck & Effective Hourly Rate

$$\text{Gross Paycheck} = \text{Regular Pay} + \text{Overtime Pay} + \text{Double-Time Pay}$$

$$\text{Total Work Hours} = \text{Regular Hours} + \text{Overtime Hours} + \text{Double Hours}$$

$$\text{Average Hourly Rate} = \frac{\text{Gross Paycheck}}{\text{Total Work Hours}}$$

### Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples (Verified Against Calculator Defaults)

#### Example 1: Standard Overtime Scenario ($25/hr Base Rate)

- **Hourly Rate**: $25.00 — **Regular Hours**: 40 hrs — **Overtime Hours**: 5 hrs (at 1.5× multiplier) — **Double Time Hours**: 0 hrs

1. **Calculate Regular Pay**: Regular Pay = 40 × $25.00 = **$1,000.00**
2. **Calculate Overtime Pay**: OT Rate = $25.00 × 1.5 = $37.50/hr; OT Pay = 5 × $37.50 = **$187.50**
3. **Calculate Gross Paycheck**: Gross Paycheck = $1,000.00 + $187.50 = **$1,187.50**
4. **Calculate Total Hours & Average Rate**: Total Hours = 40 + 5 = 45 hrs; Average Hourly Rate = $1,187.50 / 45 = **$26.39/hr**

*Verification Result: Gross Paycheck = **$1,187.50**, Total Hours = **45**, Average Rate = **$26.39/hr**, Regular Earnings = **$1,000.00**, Overtime Earnings = **$187.50**.*

---

#### Example 2: Extended Shift with Double Time ($20/hr Base Rate)

- **Hourly Rate**: $20.00 — **Regular Hours**: 40 hrs — **Overtime Hours**: 8 hrs (at 1.5×) — **Double Time Hours**: 2 hrs (Holiday / 2.0×)

1. **Regular Pay**: 40 × $20.00 = **$800.00**
2. **Overtime Pay**: 8 × ($20 × 1.5) = 8 × $30.00 = **$240.00**
3. **Double-Time Pay**: 2 × ($20 × 2.0) = 2 × $40.00 = **$80.00**
4. **Gross Paycheck**: $800.00 + $240.00 + $80.00 = **$1,120.00**
5. **Total Hours**: 40 + 8 + 2 = **50 hrs**
6. **Average Hourly Rate**: $1,120.00 / 50 hrs = **$22.40/hr**

*Verification Result: Gross Paycheck = **$1,120.00**, Total Hours = **50**, Average Rate = **$22.40/hr**, Regular Earnings = **$800.00**, Overtime Earnings = **$240.00**, Double-Time Earnings = **$80.00**.*

### A Third Worked Example (Custom 2.0x Overtime Multiplier)

Some contracts or jurisdictions pay overtime at a rate higher than the standard 1.5x. Here's a **$32.00/hr base rate**, with **40 regular hours**, **6 overtime hours at a custom 2.0x multiplier**, and **3 separate double-time hours**:

1. **Regular Pay**: 40 × $32.00 = **$1,280.00**
2. **Overtime Pay**: OT Rate = $32.00 × 2.0 = $64.00/hr; OT Pay = 6 × $64.00 = **$384.00**
3. **Double-Time Pay**: Double Rate = $32.00 × 2 = $64.00/hr; Double Pay = 3 × $64.00 = **$192.00**
4. **Gross Paycheck**: $1,280.00 + $384.00 + $192.00 = **$1,856.00**
5. **Total Hours**: 40 + 6 + 3 = **49 hrs**
6. **Average Hourly Rate**: $1,856.00 / 49 hrs = **$37.88/hr**

*Verification Result: Gross Paycheck = **$1,856.00**, Total Hours = **49**, Average Rate = **$37.88/hr**.*

> [!NOTE]
> Notice that with a 2.0x OT multiplier, the Overtime Rate and the Double-Time Rate come out identical ($64.00/hr) — but the two hour categories are still tracked and totaled separately, since they represent different pay-policy reasons for the premium even when the resulting rate happens to match.

### How Overtime Laws Vary

* **Weekly vs. Daily Overtime**: Under US Federal FLSA rules, overtime applies after working 40 hours in a single workweek. However, states like California enforce **daily overtime rules** (time-and-a-half after 8 hours in a day, double-time after 12 hours).
* **Salaried Exempt vs. Non-Exempt Workers**: Executive, administrative, and professional salaried employees meeting salary thresholds are typically "exempt" from overtime pay. Hourly workers are generally "non-exempt" and entitled to overtime.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1: What is time-and-a-half overtime?**

Time-and-a-half is the standard overtime wage rate equal to 1.5 times an employee's regular hourly pay rate. For example, an employee earning $25/hour receives $37.50 per hour for overtime hours worked.

**Q2: What is double time and when is it paid?**

Double time is a premium pay rate equal to 2.0 times an employee's regular hourly wage. It is typically paid for working on company-recognized holidays, Sundays, or after working extended daily shifts (such as over 12 consecutive hours in California), according to specific labor contracts or state regulations.

**Q3: What is the Average Hourly Rate on a paycheck?**

Average Hourly Rate is your total gross paycheck earnings divided by the total number of hours worked during that pay period. Because overtime hours are paid at higher rates, your average hourly rate will be higher than your baseline regular rate.

**Q4: Can the OT Rate Multiplier be set to something other than 1.5x?**

Yes. While 1.5x (time-and-a-half) is the FLSA-standard multiplier and the calculator's default, the OT Rate Multiplier field accepts any value, letting you model contract-specific or region-specific overtime premiums (such as a flat 2.0x rate) without touching the separate Double Time Hours field.
