# Net Worth Calculator – Personal Balance Sheet Guide

Your **Net Worth** is the single most important metric for evaluating overall financial health. It represents the net financial value left over if you sold all your assets at fair market value and paid off all outstanding debts.

This calculator computes your **total assets, total liabilities, net worth total, and asset-to-debt ratio**.

---

### How the Net Worth Calculation Engine Works

$$\mathbf{\text{Net Worth} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}}$$

#### 1. Asset Categories (+)
- **Cash & Savings**: Checking accounts, emergency funds, certificates of deposit.
- **Stocks & Mutual Funds**: Taxable brokerage accounts, equities, index funds.
- **Real Estate Valuations**: Current estimated market value of owned properties.
- **Vehicles Valuation**: Resale value of owned cars or vehicles.
- **Retirement Accounts**: 401(k), IRA, provident funds, pension accounts.

$$\text{Total Assets} = \text{Cash} + \text{Investments} + \text{Property} + \text{Vehicles} + \text{Retirement}$$

#### 2. Liability Categories (-)
- **Home Mortgage**: Remaining principal owed on property loans.
- **Car Loan Balance**: Outstanding auto financing debt.
- **Credit Card Balance**: Revolving credit card debts owed.
- **Student Loans**: Active educational loan balances.

$$\text{Total Liabilities} = \text{Mortgage} + \text{Car Loan} + \text{Credit Card} + \text{Student Loans}$$

---

### Verified Step-by-Step Worked Example

Suppose you evaluate your financial balance sheet with the following figures:

#### 1. Assets Column:
- Cash & Savings: **$20,000**
- Stocks & Mutual Funds: **$80,000**
- Real Estate Valuation: **$300,000**
- Vehicles Valuation: **$25,000**
- Retirement Accounts: **$55,000**
- **Total Assets**: 20,000 + 80,000 + 300,000 + 25,000 + 55,000 = **$480,000**

#### 2. Liabilities Column:
- Home Mortgage: **$180,000**
- Car Loan Balance: **$20,000**
- Credit Card Debt: **$5,000**
- Student Loans: **$8,000**
- **Total Liabilities**: 180,000 + 20,000 + 5,000 + 8,000 = **$213,000**

#### 3. Net Worth Calculation:
$$\text{Net Worth} = 480,000 - 213,000 = \mathbf{\$267,000}$$

---

### Second Worked Example: Negative Net Worth Scenario

An early-career professional with limited assets but recent student loan and auto debt:

**Assets**: Cash $5,000 + Investments $2,000 + Property $0 + Vehicles $8,000 + Retirement $3,000 = **$18,000**

**Liabilities**: Mortgage $0 + Car Loan $12,000 + Credit Card $4,000 + Student Loan $35,000 = **$51,000**

$$\text{Net Worth} = 18,000 - 51,000 = \mathbf{-\$33,000 \text{ (Negative)}}$$

> [!NOTE]
> A negative net worth like this is common for young professionals in the first years after graduating with student loans and is not inherently alarming - the more meaningful signal is whether net worth is trending upward over successive years as income grows and debt is repaid.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: How often should I calculate my net worth?**
  * A1: Checking your net worth once or twice a year is an effective way to track long-term wealth accumulation, monitor debt reduction, and evaluate investment growth.
* **Q2: What is considered a "good" net worth for my age?**
  * A2: A commonly cited rule of thumb suggests targeting a net worth of roughly 1x your annual salary by age 30, 3x by age 40, and 6x by age 50, though this varies widely based on income, location, and individual circumstances - it's best used as a rough benchmark, not a strict target.
* **Q3: Should I include illiquid assets like a business or collectibles in my net worth?**
  * A3: You can, but use a conservative, realistic estimate of what the asset could actually sell for, since illiquid assets (private business equity, art, collectibles) are harder to value precisely and harder to convert to cash quickly compared to liquid assets like savings or publicly traded stocks.
