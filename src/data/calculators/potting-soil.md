# Potting Soil Calculator – Calculate How Much Soil You Need

Buying potting soil without calculating volume leads to unnecessary trips to the garden center or leftover bags taking up garage space. Because commercial potting soil is packaged in various unit labels—including **Liters**, **Cubic Feet**, and **Dry Quarts**—converting container dimensions into bag counts can be confusing.

This calculator computes **Potting Soil Volume in Liters**, **Cubic Feet**, and **Dry Quarts** for **Rectangular Planters** and **Round/Cylindrical Pots**.

---

### Formulas & Volume Geometry

Soil requirements are calculated using standard solid geometry volume formulas:

#### 1. Rectangular & Square Planters (Box Volume)
$$V_{\text{cuft}} = \text{Length (ft)} \times \text{Width (ft)} \times \text{Depth (ft)}$$

#### 2. Round & Cylindrical Pots (Cylinder Volume)
$$\text{Radius (ft)} = \frac{\text{Diameter (ft)}}{2}$$
$$V_{\text{cuft}} = \pi \times [\text{Radius (ft)}]^2 \times \text{Depth (ft)}$$

#### 3. Standard Unit Conversion Constants
$$1 \text{ Cubic Foot} = 28.3168 \text{ Liters} = 25.7140 \text{ Dry Quarts}$$

### Verified Step-by-Step Worked Example

Let's calculate the soil required to fill a **rectangular raised garden bed** measuring **4 feet long**, **2 feet wide**, and **1.5 feet deep**:

#### Step 1: Calculate Volume in Cubic Feet ($V_{\text{cuft}}$)
$$V_{\text{cuft}} = 4 \text{ ft} \times 2 \text{ ft} \times 1.5 \text{ ft} = \mathbf{12.00 \text{ cubic feet}}$$

#### Step 2: Convert to Liters
$$\text{Liters} = 12.00 \times 28.3168466 = \mathbf{339.80 \text{ Liters}}$$

#### Step 3: Convert to Dry Quarts
$$\text{Dry Quarts} = 12.00 \times 25.7140463 = \mathbf{308.57 \text{ Dry Quarts}}$$

### Practical Gardening Tips: Soil Settling & Fill Levels

> [!TIP]
> - **Account for Soil Settling (Add 10%)**: Potting mix contains fluffy peat moss and perlite that compacts by **10% to 15%** after the first thorough watering. Purchase 1 extra small bag to top off planters after initial settling.
> - **Do NOT Pack Soil Tightly**: Lightly press soil around root balls, but do not stomp or compress it. Over-compacted soil destroys micro-air pockets, suffocating plant roots and impairing drainage.
> - **Farming Bulk Orders**: For large raised bed projects requiring more than 27 cubic feet (1 cubic yard), buy soil in bulk from a local landscape supplier rather than retail bags to save up to 60% on material costs.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q1: How do I calculate soil for a tapered round pot (narrower at the bottom)?**
  * A1: Measure the top diameter and bottom diameter, calculate the average diameter ($\frac{\text{Top} + \text{Bottom}}{2}$), and enter that average diameter into the calculator.
* **Q2: Should I put rocks or gravel in the bottom of pots for drainage?**
  * A2: Modern horticultural science advises **against** placing rocks at the bottom of containers. Rocks raise the "perched water table" closer to plant roots, increasing root rot risk. Use high-quality potting mix throughout the entire pot depth.
